TURBO-GP, step by step

How a measured correlation function becomes a fast correction of atmospheric turbulence in Rubin astrometry

TUrbulence Removal By fourier-Optimized Gaussian Process — companion to RTN-129 (Léget et al.), implemented in treegp.

The method: the empirical kernel of Gomes et al. 2025, inside treegp, solved on a grid with the FFT and conjugate gradient.

Astrometric residuals of one visit (one point per star, colour = displacement in mas) and the E/B-mode correlation functions of the field. The stripes are atmospheric turbulence: a pure E-mode (curl-free) field. The goal is to predict this field everywhere from the stars where it is measured.

2. A Gaussian field is its correlation function toy

A stationary Gaussian random field is entirely described by its two-point correlation function \(\xi(\mathbf{X}'-\mathbf{X}) = \mathrm{Cov}[y(\mathbf{X}), y(\mathbf{X}')]\) (Eq. 1): elongated stripes ↔ an elongated correlation map. \(\xi(0)\) is the variance. In the GP vocabulary, \(\xi\) is called the kernel.

3. What a Gaussian process does (one dimension) toy

click on the plot to add a star
\(\mathbf{C}\,\boldsymbol{\alpha} = \mathbf{y},\ \ \mathbf{C} = \mathbf{K} + \mathbf{D}\)  (Eq. 3)
\(y'(x') = \sum_{j=1}^{N} \alpha_j\, \xi(x' - x_j)\)  (Eq. 4)
Two steps. Eq. 3: solve once for the weights \(\alpha\); \(\mathbf{C}\) accounts for the redundancy between neighbouring stars and for their noise. Eq. 4: the prediction anywhere is a sum of kernel bumps, one per star, scaled by \(\alpha_j\): a convolution of the kernel with the weights placed at the stars.

5. Cleaning the measured map real visit, live

raw map × apodization window

its Fourier transform \(\hat P_k\); kept modes outlined

transformed back: the cleaned map (the kernel)

Three operations, all on a 108×108 image: (i) multiply by a window that goes smoothly to zero at a chosen radius, elliptical and matched to the anisotropy of the map; (ii) Fourier transform and zero every mode below a few times the noise, estimated from the scatter of the modes; (iii) transform back. What survives is a non-negative spectrum made of a few modes: the cleaned map is band-limited and positive semi-definite on its own grid.

6. Why a tabulated kernel is not (yet) a valid kernel toy

Bochner: \(\mathbf{K}\) is positive semi-definite for any set of positions iff the Fourier transform of \(\xi\) is non-negative everywhere. Setting the kernel to zero beyond the measured square is a truncation: it convolves the spectrum with a sinc and creates negative lobes. The matrix \(\mathbf{K}\) between the stars then has negative eigenvalues, and Cholesky fails as soon as some stars have small errors. G25 repair \(\mathbf{K}\) with an O(N³) SVD, per visit.

7. The TURBO-GP idea: put the stars on a fine grid toy

click or drag anywhere to move the star; the four bilinear weights sum to one

\(\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{v}\): the values of 40 stars deposited on their nodes, zero elsewhere

$$\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{W}\,\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{W}^T \tag{10}$$
Grid spacing \(h\) = half the pixel of the correlation map (10"). Each star is represented by the four nodes around it with bilinear weights: a sparse \(N \times N_g\) matrix \(\mathbf{W}\). Why is this exact here? The cleaned map is a finite sum of Fourier modes, so its values anywhere follow from its values on a grid; the only approximation is the bilinear smoothing at the scale \(h\).

8. On the grid, the covariance is a convolution, done with the FFT toy

input image \(\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{v}\) (stars with v = 1)

kernel image \(\kappa\) (zero beyond its support)

\(\mathbf{G}\mathbf{f} = \kappa * \mathbf{f}\): the field the weights would create at every node

↓ the real kernel image and the real weights

$$(\mathbf{G}\mathbf{f})_m = \sum_n \xi(\mathbf{g}_m - \mathbf{g}_n)\, f_n = (\kappa * \mathbf{f})_m,\qquad \mathbf{G} = \tfrac{1}{N_g}\,\mathbf{F}^\dagger\,\mathrm{diag}(\hat P_k)\,\mathbf{F} \tag{9}$$ The covariance between two nodes depends only on their separation, so the whole \(N_g \times N_g\) covariance of the nodes is a convolution: FFT, multiply by the spectrum of the kernel image, inverse FFT. Cost \(O(N_g \log N_g)\), memory \(O(N_g)\). Untick the padding to see what the periodic wrap of the FFT does.

\(\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{v} = \frac{1}{N_g}\sum_k \hat P_k\,\big|(\mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{v})_k\big|^2 \ge 0\)  (Eq. 11)

10. Applying \(\mathbf{C}\) to a vector: four steps, never a matrix toy

$$\mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{W}\Big[\mathbf{F}^{-1}\big(\hat P_k \odot \mathbf{F}\,(\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{v})\big)\Big] + \mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{v} \tag{12}$$
Cost per application: \(O(N + N_g \log N_g)\) time and \(O(N + N_g)\) memory, instead of \(O(N^3)\) and \(O(N^2)\). This is the only operation the solver needs.

  1. Deposit \(v_i\) on the four nodes around star i, with its bilinear weights: the image \(\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{v}\) (real visit: the full 1439×1454 grid, displayed on 8×8 blocks).
  2. Convolve the image by the kernel image through the padded FFT grid: the field the weights would produce at every node.
  3. Read the field back at each star by bilinear interpolation (multiply by \(\mathbf{W}\)): this is \(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{v}\).
  4. Add \(\sigma_i^2 v_i\): the result is \(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{v}\).

11. Solving \(\mathbf{C}\alpha = \mathbf{y}\) by conjugate gradient

toy 2 unknowns: minimizing \(\tfrac12 \alpha^T\mathbf{C}\alpha - \mathbf{y}^T\alpha\). Steepest descent zigzags; conjugate directions finish in 2 steps.

Start from \(\alpha_0 = 0\): the residual is the data itself, \(\mathbf{r}_0 = \mathbf{y}\), and the first direction is \(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_0\) (each star divided by its total variance). Each iteration applies \(\mathbf{C}\) once (Eq. 12), steps along the direction by the amount that minimizes the error, and builds the next direction conjugate to the previous one. Stop when \(\|\mathbf{r}\| < 10^{-7}\|\mathbf{y}\|\).

13. What it buys measured

Per component, one core, measured on this visit (markers) and extrapolated with the known scalings (N³, N² for the dense path; essentially linear for the grid). The DRP budget is 4 GB per core. On DP2: error rate 5% → 1%, total wall time 979 days → 15 days, typical memory 7 GB → 1–2 GB, and a residual E-mode 30% lower.
